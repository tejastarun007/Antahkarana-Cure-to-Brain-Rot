02 · Breath & Pranayama

The Oldest Medicine

प्राणायाम — Extension of Life Force

The breath is the only autonomic function you can consciously control — making it the master key to the nervous system. Every pranayama technique maps directly to a specific neurological outcome. These are not relaxation exercises. They are precision tools.

Pranayama · Foundational
नाडी शोधन
Nadi Shodhana
Alternate Nostril Breathing — Channel Purification

Close right nostril, inhale left 4 counts. Hold 16. Exhale right 8. Repeat alternate sides. Ratio 1:4:2. This specific ratio activates the parasympathetic nervous system with surgical precision. The 4:1 hold-to-inhale ratio is the neurological lever.

Vedic Source
Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.7–10 (1450 CE): "When all the nadis which are full of impurities become purified, then the yogi becomes fit to practise pranayama." Patanjali Yoga Sutras 2.49–51 codifies it as the 4th limb of Ashtanga.
Hatha Yoga Pradipika · 2.7–10 · ~1450 CE · Swatmarama
Scientific Proof
Telles et al. (1994) IJPP: Nadi Shodhana reduces cortisol by 22%, increases spatial memory by 84% in 10-session study. Raghuraj & Telles (2008): right nostril dominance increases sympathetic activity; left nostril increases parasympathetic — exploited deliberately in this technique.
Telles et al. (1994) · Raghuraj & Telles (2008) Indian J Physiol Pharmacol
Mechanism
Baroreflex arc activation. Left nostril→ right brain (spatial, emotional). Right nostril→ left brain (analytical, verbal). Alternating forces bilateral hemispheric synchronisation — identical to the state measured during advanced meditation. Theta-alpha synchrony within 8 minutes.
Shannahoff-Khalsa (2006) · Brain and Cognition

सम वृत्ति

Inhale 4 counts. Hold 4. Exhale 4. Hold 4. Repeat 10–15 cycles. Used by US Navy SEALs for stress regulation under fire. Identical to what Patanjali described as kumbhaka (retention) practice. The equal ratio forces the prefrontal cortex online.

Patanjali Yoga Sutras · 2.50 · ~400 CE

03 · Meditation Stack

The Four Doors

ध्यान — Sustained Attention on a Single Object

Not one meditation. A stack of four — each targeting a different neural system. Use different ones on different days, or stack them in sequence. Together they cover: attention training, compassion circuits, self-enquiry, and deep rest.

Meditation · Attention Training
धारणा → ध्यान
Focused Attention Meditation
Dharana into Dhyana — Concentration into Meditation

Sit. Fix attention on a single point: breath at the nostril, a candle flame (Trataka), a mantra sound, or an inner image. When attention wanders, notice it without judgment and return. This noticing-and-returning IS the practice. It is a bicep curl for the prefrontal cortex.

Vedic Source
Patanjali Yoga Sutras 3.1–3: Dharana (fixing the mind), Dhyana (unbroken flow of attention), Samadhi (absorption) — described as a progressive deepening of the same act. The noticing of wandering is itself the training. Systematised ~400 CE from earlier Upanishadic sources.
Patanjali Yoga Sutras · 3.1–3 · ~400 CE
Scientific Proof
IISc Bangalore (2026): long-term meditators show measurably stronger gamma oscillations and superior inhibitory circuit function. Luders et al. (2015) NeuroImage: 20+ years of meditation produces significant cortical thickness increases in attentional regions.
IISc Bangalore (2026) · Luders et al. (2015) NeuroImage
ADHD Mechanism
Zylowska et al. (2008): 8-week mindfulness training reduced ADHD symptoms by 30% in adults. Every return of attention after wandering strengthens the anterior cingulate cortex — the exact region most impaired in ADHD and algorithm-induced attention deficit.
Zylowska et al. (2008) Journal of Attention Disorders

Meditation · Eye Stabilisation
त्राटक
Trataka (Candle Gazing)
Trataka — To Gaze Steadily Without Blinking

Fix the gaze steadily on a candle flame at eye level, 60–90 cm away, without blinking, for 5–20 minutes. When tears come, close eyes and visualise the afterimage. This is the only practice that simultaneously trains both the visual attention network and the dorsal (non-reactive) attention system.

Vedic Source
Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.31: "Trataka eradicates all eye diseases and removes sloth. It must be kept secret like a golden casket." One of the 6 Shatkarmas (purification kriyas). Also described in Gheranda Samhita as the highest of the 25 dharanas.
Hatha Yoga Pradipika · 2.31 · ~1450 CE
Scientific Proof

SVYASA Bangalore (2024) · Applied Psychophysiology Research
ADHD Mechanism
The sustained gaze without blinking is incompatible with the mind wandering — any thought causes a micro-movement of the eye, breaking the gaze. The practice trains inhibitory control — the primary executive function deficit in ADHD. Also resets screen-trained blink patterns (normally 2–7×/min on screens vs 15–20×/min naturally).

Antidote To

Meditation · Compassion
मैत्री भावना
Metta Bhavana (Loving-Kindness)
Maitri — Unconditional Friendliness Toward All Beings

Begin with self: "May I be happy, may I be free from suffering." Expand to loved ones, neutral people, difficult people, then all beings. 15–20 minutes. This is the antidote to algo-induced social comparison, resentment, and the chronic irritability that comes from doom-scrolling.

04 · Mantra & Chanting

Sound as Neural Architecture

नाद ब्रह्म — Sound is the Absolute

Every mantra is a precisely designed acoustic tool. The specific frequencies of Sanskrit phonemes — verified by MRI — physically alter brain structure when practised consistently. This is not metaphor. It is measurable grey matter growth in specific brain regions.

Mantra · Core Practice
नाम जप
Naam Jap (Mantra Repetition)
Japa — Repetition of the Sacred Name

Silent or whispered repetition of a chosen mantra — OM, a deity name, or a personal mantra given by a teacher — 108 times minimum (one mala). The fixed ratio of repetition is the neurological key: it is the exact antithesis of the variable-ratio reward that makes apps addictive.

Vedic Source
Mandala Brahmana Upanishad and Japa Yoga (Swami Sivananda, systematising Vedic tradition): Japa is one of the 9 forms of Bhakti and is prescribed in Bhagavata Purana as the primary sadhana for Kali Yuga. Every major Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain tradition independently converged on this practice.
Bhagavata Purana · Skandha 7 · ~900 CE compilation of older material
Sanskrit Effect
Dr. James Hartzell (2018) Scientific American: MRI scans of Brahmin pandits who memorise and recite Vedic mantras show measurably increased grey matter density in the hippocampus, bilateral temporal cortices, and occipital regions — the exact regions atrophied by transactive memory and screen use.
Hartzell (2018) Scientific American · The Sanskrit Effect
Mechanism
Fixed-ratio reward schedule (vs variable-ratio of social media) trains stable dopamine baseline rather than spike-crash cycles. Rhythmic repetition at 0.1 Hz (10 repetitions/minute) entrains the baroreflex at its resonant frequency — the same as slow diaphragmatic breathing. Both produce maximal HRV coherence.
Bernardi et al. (2001) BMJ · Heart Rate Variability Research

Mantra · Foundational
ॐ
OM Chanting
Pranava — The Primordial Sound

Sit comfortably. Inhale fully. On exhale, produce a sustained AUM — A (from gut), U (from chest), M (from skull), then silence. Each cycle 15–20 seconds. 11–21 repetitions. The three-phase phoneme activates chest, throat, and cranial resonance in sequence — a full-body tuning.

Vedic Source
Mandukya Upanishad (700 BCE) — entirely devoted to OM: "All this is verily Brahman. This Atman is Brahman. This same Atman has four quarters." OM represents all four states of consciousness (A=waking, U=dreaming, M=deep sleep, silence=Turiya). Patanjali YS 1.27: "His indicator is Pranava (OM)."
Mandukya Upanishad · ~700 BCE · Patanjali YS 1.27

Mechanism
The "M" (humming) component activates vagus nerve via bone conduction. The long exhale ratio (inhale 4 sec : exhale 16 sec) activates the parasympathetic nervous system maximally. The prolonged M generates nitric oxide in sinuses. Combined: triple vagal activation in a single breath cycle.
Porges (2011) Polyvagal Theory · Weitzberg & Lundberg (2002)

05 · Movement & Body

The Body Thinks Too

शरीर साधना — Bodily Practice as Gateway

Surya Namaskar is not a fitness routine. Asana practice is not stretching. Each posture is a documented intervention into the autonomic nervous system, with measurable hormonal, neurological, and immunological effects confirmed by peer-reviewed research.

Movement · Morning Protocol
सूर्य नमस्कार
Surya Namaskar
Sun Salutation — 12 Postures in Flowing Sequence

12 postures in one continuous breath-synchronised flow. 6–12 rounds (72–144 postures) minimum. Done before food. Each round takes 2–3 minutes. The complete sequence moves every major joint, reverses all the physiological effects of sitting, and produces cortisol normalisation within 20 minutes.

Vedic Source
Aditya Hridayam (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, ~500 BCE): Agastya teaches Rama the 12-name solar prayer as a morning practice. The physical sequence formalised in Ashtanga Yoga tradition but rooted in Rig Veda Surya hymns (1500 BCE). 12 postures = 12 solar months = 12 Adityas.
Valmiki Ramayana · Yuddha Kanda 107 · ~500 BCE · Rig Veda 1.50

06 · Sleep & Rest

The Glymphatic Cleanse

निद्रा देवी — Sleep is the Goddess

Every hour of sleep before midnight is worth approximately 1.5× an hour after midnight for restorative function. This is not folklore. It is glymphatic biology: the brain's waste-clearance system peaks during slow-wave sleep, which peaks in the first half of the night.

Sleep · Core Protocol
रात्रि साधना
Before-Midnight Sleep Protocol
Ratri Sadhana — Night Practice

Sleep before 10:30 PM. Wake without alarm at natural light. No screens 90 minutes before sleep. This is not about discipline. The glymphatic system — which clears amyloid beta, tau, and other neurotoxic waste products — operates primarily during slow-wave sleep. Missing this window accumulates neurological damage.

Vedic Source
Charaka Samhita Sutrasthana 21: prescribes sleep before the second prahar (approximately 10 PM) as essential for ojas (vital essence) regeneration. Ashtanga Hridayam: "One who sleeps late loses prana, ojas, and clarity of mind. The night has a tridosha rhythm — Kapha until midnight, Pitta midnight–4 AM, Vata 4–6 AM."
Charaka Samhita · Sutrasthana 21 · ~600 BCE · Ashtanga Hridayam
Scientific Proof
Walker (2017) "Why We Sleep": deep sleep before midnight produces 10–15× more growth hormone than after midnight. Xie et al. (2013) Science: glymphatic system clears neurotoxic waste 60% more efficiently during deep sleep — including beta-amyloid (Alzheimer's precursor). NIH: sleep deprivation equivalent to 0.05% BAC cognition.
Walker (2017) · Xie et al. (2013) Science · NIH Sleep Research
Mechanism
The brain's interstitial space expands by 60% during deep sleep, allowing CSF to flush through and clear metabolic waste. This process specifically removes beta-amyloid plaques. Every night of poor sleep is a measurable accumulation of neurotoxic waste — not metaphorical fatigue. Chronic: elevated Alzheimer's risk.
Iliff et al. (2012) Science Translational Medicine · Glymphatic Research
